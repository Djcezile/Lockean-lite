# M6 comparative strategy research

All five hypotheses are unproven for Lockean. The decision ranks future research
work. Empirical admission, current tradability and all-in profitability are unresolved.

## Scoring framework


Score each criterion independently on 0–3. Use 0 for unavailable evidence or a material blocker, 1 for limited support or substantial friction, 2 for credible support or manageable conditional friction, and 3 for strong support or comparatively straightforward feasibility. A zero marked U means unresolved rather than poor economics. No imputation or renormalization. Confidence is reported separately. Scores prioritize diligence and convey no expected return. If second place ties under the balanced weights, report INCONCLUSIVE. Top-two instability under sensitivity must be disclosed.

## Candidate records

### Trend-following momentum

A slow response to information or persistent positioning may make an instrument's
own past return predict its next return. R5 documents historical persistence across
futures and forwards. R6 challenges the contribution of the trend signal once volatility
scaling is separated. A liquid US ETF study is a transfer hypothesis. Whipsaws, rapid
reversals, long-only exposure and volatility scaling can explain or erase apparent value.

A small point-in-time universe of unlevered equity and bond ETFs uses completed daily
bars, distributions and splits as known, listings and delistings, and executable quotes
at the later scheduled decision. ETF inception and closure must remain in the historical
universe. The longer decision window lowers latency and monitoring demands. Bid/ask,
commissions, turnover during choppy regimes and missed or partial fills still matter.
Liquid funds may permit scaling at modest participation, subject to depth and crowding.
The simpler joins and scheduled proposals support the stronger P/X/R/O/F ratings.
Commercial data rights, actual spend and capacity are unresolved.

### Post-earnings announcement drift

Investor underreaction could leave subsequent sector-adjusted returns aligned with
the earnings surprise. R3 and R4 support historical drift and sensitivity to the
surprise definition. R7 shows that liquidity and trading costs can absorb the effect.
Results may depend on reporting regime, volatility and availability of analyst coverage.

Use listed US common shares, with a liquidity screen fixed from prior data. Daily prices
alone are insufficient. Preserve as-published EPS and its accounting basis, pre-release
consensus vintages, first release and receipt, corrections, after-hours calendar,
corporate actions, delistings and stable issuer-to-security mappings. Entry at a later
eligible session reduces dependence on first-response speed. Event clustering, gap
risk, sector crowding and illiquid names constrain capacity. Use long/cash first.
Shorting would add availability, borrow and financing gates. A scheduled event queue
fits independent authorization. The extra joins, rights and latency evidence make it
more expensive to investigate than ETF trend.

### Intraday ETF momentum

Flows or hedging demand could carry early returns into the final half-hour. R10 reports
an early-to-late relation in ETFs, strongest in some high-volume or volatile regimes.
R11 finds broader intraday continuation with a different predictor and attributes part
of it to hedging demand. That difference weakens transfer of the exact early-window rule.

Start with one predeclared broad ETF and require a full calendar, historical membership
for any extension, one-minute completed bars, ordered trades and contemporaneous quotes.
Keep raw auction flags, corporate actions, DST, half-days and correction/receipt times.
Capacity may be plausible in liquid funds but the execution window and close auction
concentrate impact and approval latency. Monitor halts and partial fills, and test a
fallback that skips stale proposals. Data volume and time-sensitive operations lower
execution and burden scores despite simpler joins than earnings or news.

### Short-term mean reversion

Temporary liquidity pressure may reverse after a sharp residual move. R8 relates
reversal speed and persistence to volatility and turnover. R9 finds short-term reversal
especially sensitive to trading costs. In stressed or information-driven markets a
move may continue. Spread bounce and stale quotes can create apparent predictability.

Use liquid US common stocks with completed daily observations, quote-level execution
evidence, prior-day eligibility, splits, dividends, delistings and event exclusions
known at decision. Cross-sectional joins, gap exposure, high turnover, adverse selection,
fees and spread/impact burden the result. The names with strongest reversal may have
the least usable depth. A scheduled proposal is possible, but delay sensitivity and
daily basket monitoring weaken architecture fit and commercial scale. Intraday variants
would add a different latency and licensing profile and require separate approval.

### News-reaction trading

Novel, directional company news may be incorporated with delay. R1 studies daily
media pessimism and subsequent reversal rather than a general stock-level headline
continuation rule. R2 finds that automated analytics accelerate the reaction and can
reduce liquidity. That creates a credible mechanism and an explicit reason a slower
strategy could fail, especially on crowded announcements and volatile days.

Limit the design to liquid US common shares and a predeclared class of non-earnings
corporate news. Archive first publication, provider arrival, local availability, story
versions, ticker mappings, deduplication, halts and point-in-time universe and actions.
Require timestamped quotes and trades near each decision, plus versioned content or
features. Speed, adverse selection, headline bursts and source entitlements raise
recurring costs and constrain scaling. Authorization delay may consume the usable
window. Continuous ingestion and monitoring are the heaviest burden in the comparison.
