**Mode:** Alpaca paper, controlled active entries

**Purpose:** Repeat the Day 16 loss-loop validation under an unchanged policy,
measure AI behavior after a completed directional cooldown, and identify any
remaining difference between entry containment and total realized stop risk.

## Outcome

Day 17 completed automatically after the Alpaca market closed. Authorization,
broker reconciliation, stop management, directional cooldown, the two-stop
new-entry halt, end-of-day cleanup, and clean termination all passed. No broker,
market-data, AI, position-exit, or session-state failure occurred.

The session also exposed an important policy boundary. The second confirmed
stop correctly halted every later new entry, but one separately authorized
spread was already open. That residual spread remained under normal risk
management and later stopped, producing three confirmed stopped spreads even
though the new-entry halt threshold was two. This matches the implemented
contract; it does not guarantee a maximum of two eventual stopped positions.

## Policy Timeline

1. At the opening cycle, the AI chose a bullish call spread even though only
   trend passed; momentum, breakout, and volatility failed, and intraday data
   was not yet available.
2. The spread stopped. Broker history reconstructed one bullish stop and
   activated the 3,600-second bullish cooldown.
3. During the cooldown, nine bullish trade recommendations were rejected before
   broker submission. The AI independently declined one ambiguous setup.
4. When the directional cooldown expired, a new bullish spread was authorized.
5. Two later proposals were rejected because their sell-to-open legs conflicted
   with the existing long position.
6. A structurally compatible second bullish spread was then authorized. The
   portfolio remained within the two-committed-spread cap.
7. That newer spread stopped first. Its confirmed fill raised the session count
   to two and activated the hard new-entry halt.
8. The older open spread remained managed. It later stopped, raising the broker-
   reconstructed count to three without any new entry occurring after the halt.
9. The runner blocked all later entries, continued risk checks and end-of-day
   cleanup, and closed flat with no pending or unknown order.

## What Day 17 Proved

- The first-stop directional cooldown repeated correctly on a second live day.
- The AI could not bypass the cooldown despite nine same-direction proposals.
- Cooldown expiry restored eligibility exactly as designed.
- Independent structural checks rejected two incompatible proposals.
- The two-stop halt prevented all new exposure after its activation.
- Existing-position exit authority remained active during the hard halt.
- The broker-derived counter continued from two to three when the residual
  position stopped; no state was lost or reset.
- The entire session reconciled without unknown orders, stale cancellations,
  failures, or tracebacks.

## What Day 17 Did Not Prove

- It did not demonstrate a profitable entry edge. Every submitted spread lost.
- It did not exercise a take-profit fill under the loss-loop policy.
- It did not exercise pending-entry cancellation when a restriction activates.
- It did not intentionally restart the runner mid-session.
- It did not prove that a two-stop new-entry halt is equivalent to a maximum of
  two eventual stopped positions; Day 17 proved that those are different rules.

## Strategy Finding

The AI produced fourteen `TRADE` judgments and only one `NO_TRADE` judgment.
Every trade judgment was bullish. The daily trend signal repeatedly outweighed
failed daily momentum, breakout, and volatility signals, and sometimes mixed
intraday momentum. The deterministic controls safely contained that behavior,
but the evidence reinforces that `active_paper` is an evidence-generation mode,
not a mature profitable strategy.

Across Days 16 and 17 under the new containment policy, five submitted spreads
all stopped for a combined -$33.50. That sample is too small for statistical
optimization, but it is sufficient to reject any claim that the current entry
logic has already demonstrated positive expectancy.

## Decision Gate

The current contract is: halt **new entries** after two confirmed stops and
continue normally managing positions that were already authorized. If the
intended contract is instead: permit no more than two eventual stopped spreads
in a session, the minimum non-liquidating change is to make remaining stop-loss
capacity an exposure gate. After one confirmed stop, only one committed spread
could exist; after two, none could be added. That would prevent residual
concurrent exposure from producing a third stopped spread without forcibly
closing an otherwise healthy position.

This changes the risk meaning of the policy and therefore requires an explicit
decision before the next active-paper session.
